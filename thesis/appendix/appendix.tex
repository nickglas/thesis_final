\section{Stage~K (Cross-Node Alternative): kind Multi-Worker CNI Sensitivity Data}
\label{sec:appendix-rq14b}

This section holds the full data tables for the Stage~K (cross-node) kind
multi-worker CNI sensitivity stage; a summary of the salient figures appears in
the main deployment evaluation (\cref{sec:rq14b-stub}). The methodological framing of this
stage is given in \cref{sec:methods-rq14b}; the analytical reading and
its scope-bounding role in the deployment narrative are in
\cref{sec:analysis-rq14b}. As established in \cref{sec:methods-rq14b},
Stage~K (cross-node) is a supporting sensitivity condition, not a
primary thesis-facing number: kind's worker ``nodes'' are Docker
containers sharing one host kernel and communicating over kind's CNI
bridge, so Stage~K (cross-node) measures CNI-bridge cost on a single physical host
rather than real cross-host networking. The genuine multi-host
inter-node penalty is measured separately in \cref{sec:rq15b} on AKS.

Functional parity was verified independently of timing:
\texttt{max\_abs\_diff}~$=0.0$ at \texttt{atol}~$=1\times10^{-5}$ for
all five conditions in both the local segment validation and the live
gRPC chain validation (\texttt{num\_inputs}~$=5$), recorded in
\texttt{merged\_results/parity\_validation.json}. Infrastructure
controls (image
\texttt{thesis-inference:latest} with pull policy
\texttt{IfNotPresent}, namespace \texttt{rq14b}, per-pod 1~CPU /
512~MiB Guaranteed-QoS service profile, per-pod 1~CPU / 1~GiB client
profile, one thread per pool, CPU affinity to core~0) were applied
uniformly across the five conditions, as preserved in
\texttt{merged\_results/deployment\_metadata.json} and
\texttt{merged\_results/environment.json}. Host-level controls were
weaker than in Stage~K: writes to \texttt{/sys} failed because those
paths were read-only in the containerised environment, the detected
governor was \texttt{schedutil} (rather than \texttt{performance}),
turbo was enabled, and the \texttt{nice~-5} request failed for
permission reasons (\texttt{environment.json}). This degraded
host-control state is one reason \cref{sec:analysis-rq14b} interprets
the comparison against Stage~K at the per-condition delta level only and
treats Stage~K (cross-node) as a kind-CNI sensitivity envelope rather than a clean
topology-only swap from Stage~K.

\section{Sample Generative AI Prompts}
\label{sec:appendix-ai-prompts}

This appendix gives representative examples of the prompts used during the
AI-assisted steps disclosed in \cref{sec:methods-ai}. They are included to make the
nature and the limits of that assistance transparent. In every case the output was
treated as a suggestion to be verified by the author rather than as content to be used
unchecked: surfaced references and data points were confirmed against their primary
sources before they were relied upon, and edited text was reviewed so that no claim,
number, or citation was introduced that the author had not independently established.

\paragraph{Language editing (Claude 4.7 / 4.8).}
\begin{quote}
\itshape ``Check the following paragraph from my thesis for grammar, spelling,
punctuation, and sentence structure, and suggest clearer phrasings. Do not change the
technical meaning, do not alter any reported numbers, and do not add claims I have not
made. [author's drafted paragraph pasted here]''
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Literature discovery (Perplexity / Consensus).}
\begin{quote}
\itshape ``I am looking for peer-reviewed papers on the latency and resource overhead of
sidecar-based mutual TLS in Kubernetes service meshes. List candidate papers with their
title, venue, and year so that I can locate and read the originals myself.''
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Locating evidence within a known paper (ChatGPT 5.5 / Claude 4.7).}
\begin{quote}
\itshape ``In the attached paper, which section or table reports the measured mTLS
latency overhead, and what is the reported value? Point me to where it appears so that I
can verify it against the source.''
\end{quote}

\subsection{RQ1.5b: AKS Multi-Node Sensitivity}
\label{sec:analysis-rq15b}

RQ1.5b asks how much additional end-to-end latency the inter-node network path
adds when every chain hop of the predefined ResNet-18 chain family is forced
across an AKS node boundary, relative to the same-day single-node RQ1.5
baseline. The analytical contribution is not to introduce a second primary
deployment number on top of \cref{sec:analysis-rq15}, and not to reselect a
split point, but to convert the single-node-placement validity threat that
\cref{sec:analysis-rq15} acknowledges into a measured per-condition bound, and
to test whether the RQ1.4 / RQ1.5 architectural ordering and the
activation-driven bounded-non-linearity pattern survive once the chain
traffic actually crosses the Azure VNet. The natural reporting discipline
for this kind of sensitivity comparison is ranking + cross-round consistency
rather than millisecond identity~\cite{benchmarking_sc15}.

\paragraph{Finding 1: Multi-node placement preserves the AKS condition ordering and the bounded-non-linearity pattern.}
With every chain segment of a condition forced onto a distinct AKS node and
the benchmark client on a sixth dedicated node, mean end-to-end latency
increases monotonically from 73.108~ms for
\texttt{monolithic\_k8s\_1svc} to 76.470~ms, 77.541~ms, 82.103~ms, and
84.605~ms for \texttt{chain\_2svc} through \texttt{chain\_5svc}
(\cref{tab:rq15b_results}). The bounded-non-linearity pattern from RQ1.4 and
RQ1.5 also reproduces under multi-node execution: the
\texttt{chain\_4svc}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{chain\_5svc} increment is
+2.502~ms, smaller than the preceding +4.562~ms
\texttt{chain\_3svc}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{chain\_4svc} step
(\cref{tab:rq15b_results}). This is consistent with the smaller 98~KiB
\texttt{layer4} output transferred at the added boundary --- the
activation-transfer dimension of the RQ1.3 two-dimensional
account~\cite{deeperthings} reappearing now in a fourth substrate, after
\texttt{127.0.0.1} (RQ1.1), local \texttt{kind} (RQ1.4), and single-node AKS
(RQ1.5). The architectural ordering and its shape are therefore not
artefacts of single-node colocation on AKS.

\paragraph{Finding 2: The inter-node penalty is positive and bounded.}
\Cref{tab:rq15b_delta} reports the condition-by-condition multi-node penalty
$\Delta_c = \mathrm{mean}_c^{\mathrm{multi}} - \mathrm{mean}_c^{\mathrm{single}}$
against the frozen single-node RQ1.5 baseline. The monolithic condition
gains +2.305~ms relative to the single-node monolithic. This is compatible
with the 588~KiB input tensor ($1\times3\times224\times224$ FP32) moving
across the Azure VNet rather than over a loopback socket once the benchmark
client is forced onto a dedicated node; layered RPC stacks are known to add
non-trivial transport, copying, and serialisation work on top of payload
bytes themselves~\cite{rpc_overhead_hotos25}, although RQ1.5b does not
decompose the 2.305~ms into stack-layer sub-shares. The chained conditions
add per-condition deltas of +3.033~ms (\texttt{chain\_2svc}), +2.131~ms
(\texttt{chain\_3svc}), +3.808~ms (\texttt{chain\_4svc}), and +4.086~ms
(\texttt{chain\_5svc}). The multi-node penalty is therefore a measured upper
bound rather than an open acknowledgement: on the evaluated chain family on
this AKS configuration, the worst-case single-node-to-multi-node delta is
+4.086~ms (\texttt{chain\_5svc}), and the monolithic baseline alone gains
+2.305~ms.

\paragraph{Finding 2a: The per-condition penalty is not monotonic in hop count.}
The chained-condition deltas do not increase smoothly with the number of
inter-node hops: \texttt{chain\_3svc} (+2.131~ms) carries a smaller penalty
than \texttt{chain\_2svc} (+3.033~ms), while \texttt{chain\_4svc} (+3.808~ms)
and \texttt{chain\_5svc} (+4.086~ms) sit above both. Two readings are
defensible at this evidence level. First, the magnitudes are small relative
to the within-condition standard deviations in
\cref{tab:rq15b_results} (e.g. 2.786~ms for \texttt{chain\_3svc} and
3.435~ms for \texttt{chain\_2svc}), so part of the non-monotonicity is
compatible with managed-cloud measurement variability rather than a
systematic per-hop mechanism; public-cloud network variability is known to
shift absolute latency without changing the underlying application
behaviour~\cite{noise_in_clouds,noisy_neighbor_k8s}, and the per-condition
pod-to-node assignments differ across conditions
(\texttt{merged\_results/deployment\_metadata.json}). Second, the
qualitative reading from RQ1.4 carries over: the marginal cost of an
additional inter-node hop is not a constant surcharge, so summarising
RQ1.5b as a single ``per-hop multi-node cost'' would over-read the run.
The defensible summary is the \emph{envelope}
+2.131~ms\,..\,+4.086~ms across the chained conditions and the
\emph{worst case} +4.086~ms at \texttt{chain\_5svc}, not a fitted per-hop
slope.

\paragraph{Finding 3: Round-to-round consistency is preserved across nodes.}
Per-condition round CVs in RQ1.5b are 0.0036--0.0127 for the chained
conditions and 0.0084 for the monolithic baseline
(\cref{tab:rq15b_rounds}), comparable to and in some cases tighter than the
single-node RQ1.5 chained CVs of 0.0019--0.0061 and the RQ1.5 monolithic
CV of 0.0117 (\cref{tab:rq15_rounds}). By the SC'15 reporting
standard~\cite{benchmarking_sc15}, this places RQ1.5b in the same
within-run-stability regime as the single-node RQ1.5 primary; the
distributed placement did not introduce large round-to-round drift in this
frozen run. The round CV is a within-run stability measure and does not
bound cross-cluster or cross-run replication; that remains explicitly out
of scope (\cref{sec:methods-rq15b}).

\paragraph{Finding 4: kind multi-worker is not a substitute for the RQ1.5b measurement.}
Read together with \cref{sec:analysis-rq14b}, the RQ1.5b per-condition
deltas (+2.131~ms\,..\,+4.086~ms across the chained conditions,
\cref{tab:rq15b_delta}) are roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the
corresponding kind multi-worker deltas of +3.991~ms\,..\,+48.634~ms
reported in \cref{tab:rq14b_delta}. The two stages also differ in
host-control state, so the comparison is qualitative rather than a clean
isolation of CNI cost; CNI plugin choice and the pod-network path are
nonetheless an independent and well-documented source of measured-latency
variance in Kubernetes clusters~\cite{cni_k8s_ic2e21}, and absolute timings
are known not to transfer cleanly across container, VM, and managed-cluster
substrates~\cite{virtualization_costs,managed_k8s_perf}. The analytical
consequence for the deployment strand is therefore that RQ1.4b should be
read as a kind-CNI sensitivity envelope on the single-node RQ1.4 baseline,
and RQ1.5b should be read as the credible cross-host bound on the
single-node RQ1.5 baseline; treating the RQ1.4b numbers as a cross-host
estimate would inflate the inferred inter-node cost by an order of
magnitude relative to what AKS actually measures here.

\paragraph{Validation.}
Four pieces of evidence support treating the per-condition multi-node
penalty as credible within the bounds RQ1.5b sets. First, functional parity
holds: both the local segment-chain check and the live in-cluster gRPC
chain check report \texttt{max\_abs\_diff}~$=0.0$ at the predeclared
tolerance of $1.0\times10^{-5}$ across all five conditions
(\texttt{merged\_results/parity\_validation.json}), so the per-condition
means and deltas are not confounded by numerical drift across the
partitioned graph. Second, multi-node placement was enforced and verified
post-deployment: per-condition pod placements in
\texttt{merged\_results/deployment\_metadata.json} are pairwise disjoint
across nodes and the benchmark client is always on a node distinct from
every service pod, and the run-level
\texttt{multi\_node\_validation.passed=True} entry in
\texttt{merged\_results/environment.json} aggregates that check
(\cref{sec:methods-rq15b}). Third, the AKS leg is byte-exact reproducible
at the application-code level: every RQ1.5b condition ran with the same
pinned ACR image digest used in RQ1.5
(\texttt{sha256:d292aef407ee8a15da2b}\allowbreak%
\texttt{daa680de1cc24b0975e7de81d27e1fc3}\allowbreak%
\texttt{a7d128b8736b}) under image pull policy \texttt{Always}
(\cref{sec:methods-artifacts}), so the single-node-to-multi-node delta is
not contaminated by application-code drift between the two stages. Fourth,
by the SC'15 reporting standard the cross-round CV range 0.0036--0.0127 is
in the same stability regime as the single-node RQ1.5 frozen
run~\cite{benchmarking_sc15}. Five limits must be stated explicitly. (i)
RQ1.5b is one AKS cluster, one node SKU (\texttt{Standard\_D8s\_v3}), one
region (\texttt{swedencentral}), one node pool (\texttt{rq15bpool}), one
CNI (\texttt{kubenet}), one fixed seed; cross-SKU, cross-region, cross-CNI,
or cross-vendor replication is not in scope, and absolute managed-cloud
performance is known to shift with each of those
axes~\cite{managed_k8s_perf,virtualization_costs,cni_k8s_ic2e21}. (ii) The
benchmark client's \texttt{nice~-5} request and CPU-governor / turbo
controls could not be applied inside the AKS pod environment
(\texttt{merged\_results/environment.json}: \texttt{priority.applied=false},
\texttt{governor.detected=unavailable}); host-control state is therefore
weaker than in RQ1.4 and matches the same caveat declared for RQ1.5. (iii)
Per-condition pod-to-node assignments differ across conditions, so the
small non-monotonicity in the per-condition deltas
(\cref{tab:rq15b_delta}) is compatible with run-internal placement-locality
variability and is not isolated to chain depth. (iv) The +2.305~ms
monolithic-baseline gain is interpreted at the mechanism level (VNet vs
loopback for the 588~KiB input); the run does not decompose this gain into
transport, copy, serialisation, or kernel-network sub-shares, and
\cite{rpc_overhead_hotos25} measures the RPC-tax share in different
workloads, so it supports the qualitative mechanism, not the specific
2.305~ms magnitude. (v) The run is one execution of the chain family on
this cluster; round-level stability bounds within-run variability but not
across-run cluster-level replication.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
The evaluative reading of RQ1.5b has three facets. First, against the
analytical purpose of the stage --- bounding the inter-node cost that the
single-node RQ1.5 design intentionally hides --- the result is sufficient.
On this AKS configuration the worst-case multi-node penalty across the
chained conditions is +4.086~ms (\texttt{chain\_5svc},
\cref{tab:rq15b_delta}) and the monolithic baseline gains +2.305~ms; the
single-node RQ1.4 / RQ1.5 design therefore underestimates true
production-style latency by a bounded margin that is now quantified per
condition rather than acknowledged abstractly. Second, against the RQ1.5
ordering and bounded-non-linearity claims, the result is confirmatory:
\cref{tab:rq15b_results} reproduces the RQ1.5 ordering and the
activation-driven last-step pattern under enforced cross-host placement,
which strengthens the RQ1.3 / RQ1.5 architectural reading without
extending it beyond the evaluated coarse-stage ResNet-18 chain family.
Third, against the boundaries of what RQ1.5b can claim, the result is not
sufficient to support production-readiness, scalability, multi-tenant
robustness, autoscaling, alternate-SKU, alternate-region, alternate-CNI,
or millisecond-portability claims. Concurrent clients, service-mesh
hardening, autoscaling, alternate node SKUs and regions, and alternate
CNI choices are out of scope for RQ1.5b; the service-mesh-hardening cost
is addressed in the RQ2.1 / RQ2.1b strand, and managed-cloud absolute
drift across substrates is well-documented
independently~\cite{managed_k8s_perf,virtualization_costs,cni_k8s_ic2e21,%
noise_in_clouds}. The contribution of RQ1.5b stops at the inter-node-cost
boundary it was designed to measure.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.5b.}
Under enforced multi-node placement on the same AKS cluster, the same
five-condition predefined chain family preserves the monotonic overhead
progression seen in RQ1.4 and RQ1.5: per-condition mean latency increases
from 73.108~ms (\texttt{monolithic\_k8s\_1svc}) to 84.605~ms
(\texttt{chain\_5svc}, \cref{tab:rq15b_results}), and the
\texttt{chain\_4svc}~$\rightarrow$~\texttt{chain\_5svc} step is +2.502~ms
against a preceding +4.562~ms step, in line with the smaller 98~KiB
\texttt{layer4} activation transferred at the added boundary. The
per-condition multi-node penalty relative to the single-node RQ1.5
baseline ranges from +2.131~ms to +4.086~ms across the chained conditions
(\cref{tab:rq15b_delta}) and is +2.305~ms for the monolithic baseline; the
penalty is not monotonic in hop count, with \texttt{chain\_3svc} sitting
below \texttt{chain\_2svc} at this run's pod placements, and is best
summarised as an envelope rather than a fitted per-hop slope. Cross-round
CVs are 0.0036--0.0127 (\cref{tab:rq15b_rounds}), in the same stability
regime as the single-node RQ1.5 frozen run~\cite{benchmarking_sc15}.
Read against \cref{sec:analysis-rq14b}, these per-condition deltas are an
order of magnitude smaller than the kind multi-worker CNI-bridge deltas
of +3.991~ms\,..\,+48.634~ms (\cref{tab:rq14b_delta}), which confirms
that kind multi-worker is a CNI sensitivity envelope rather than a
substitute for the managed-cloud cross-host bound that RQ1.5b actually
measures~\cite{cni_k8s_ic2e21,virtualization_costs}. The single-node
RQ1.4 / RQ1.5 design therefore underestimates true production latency by
a bounded margin that is now quantified per condition on this AKS
configuration; RQ1.5b does not support production-readiness, scalability,
or cross-substrate-portability claims.
